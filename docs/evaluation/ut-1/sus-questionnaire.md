# System Usability Scale

The ten standard items (Brooke, 1996), unchanged except that "system" is the
platform as a whole. Administered at the end of every session, **before** any
discussion of what went wrong, because discussing the problems first would move
the answers.

## Instructions to the participant

> These are ten statements about the platform you have just used. For each one,
> mark how much you agree, from 1 "strongly disagree" to 5 "strongly agree".
> There is no right answer and it is the platform being judged, not you. Answer
> quickly — your first reaction is what is wanted. If you cannot decide about a
> statement, mark 3 and move on.

## The items

| # | Statement | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | I think that I would like to use this system frequently. | positive |
| 2 | I found the system unnecessarily complex. | negative |
| 3 | I thought the system was easy to use. | positive |
| 4 | I think that I would need the support of a technical person to be able to use this system. | negative |
| 5 | I found the various functions in this system were well integrated. | positive |
| 6 | I thought there was too much inconsistency in this system. | negative |
| 7 | I would imagine that most people would learn to use this system very quickly. | positive |
| 8 | I found the system very cumbersome to use. | negative |
| 9 | I felt very confident using the system. | positive |
| 10 | I needed to learn a lot of things before I could get going with this system. | negative |

The odd items are worded positively and the even items negatively, on purpose:
it stops a participant from ticking straight down one column.

## Scoring

Each item is answered 1 to 5.

1. **Odd items (1, 3, 5, 7, 9):** contribution = answer − 1.
2. **Even items (2, 4, 6, 8, 10):** contribution = 5 − answer.
3. Add the ten contributions (0–40) and **multiply by 2.5**.

The result is a number from 0 to 100. It is **not a percentage** and not a
percentile: it is a score on the SUS scale, and calling it "79 %" in the
write-up would be wrong.

### Worked example

Answers 4, 2, 4, 1, 4, 2, 5, 2, 4, 2:

| Item | Answer | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (odd) | 4 | 3 |
| 2 (even) | 2 | 3 |
| 3 (odd) | 4 | 3 |
| 4 (even) | 1 | 4 |
| 5 (odd) | 4 | 3 |
| 6 (even) | 2 | 3 |
| 7 (odd) | 5 | 4 |
| 8 (even) | 2 | 3 |
| 9 (odd) | 4 | 3 |
| 10 (even) | 2 | 3 |
| **Sum** | | **32** |

32 × 2.5 = **80**.

## Reading the number

The usual interpretation, from the published norms across hundreds of studies:
about **68** is average; above 80 is good; below 50 is poor. Two cautions for
this study:

- With five to ten participants the mean carries real uncertainty. Report the
  mean, the sample standard deviation **and every individual score**, and do not
  compare a difference of a few points between rounds as if it were meaningful.
- SUS measures perceived usability of the whole system in one number. It cannot
  say *what* was wrong — that is what the observation sheets are for. A high
  score alongside a failed task is not a contradiction; it is the participant
  being polite about a real problem, and the task outcome is the harder
  evidence.
