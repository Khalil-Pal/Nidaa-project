# UT-1 task script — psychologist

**Read to the participant, one task at a time. Do not read ahead, do not explain
the interface, and do not name a button, a page or a menu item.**

Sign-in details are on the facilitator's sheet (`ut-psy1@example.test`; the
account is approved and professionally verified, and starts **off duty**). Start
signed out. The setup script has left one waiting case, and an anonymous case
already assigned to this psychologist with no session recorded yet.

---

## Warm-up (not scored)

> You are a psychologist who has joined the platform to take a few cases. Sign
> in and tell me what you think you are looking at.

---

## Task 1 — Make yourself available

> You have two hours free this afternoon and you are willing to take an urgent
> case. Make that true on the platform.

**Done when:** the account is on duty.

Watch for: whether the distinction between being approved, being verified and
being on duty is legible; whether they expect going on duty to be a schedule
rather than a switch; whether anything tells them what being on duty means (that
a crisis case can be routed to them automatically). Ask afterwards: "what do you
think will happen now?"

---

## Task 2 — Take a case

> Someone is waiting for support. Take their case.

**Done when:** the waiting case is assigned to them.

Watch for: whether they can tell how urgent the case is and why; whether they
look for the person's name and what they conclude when it is not there; whether
they know how the conversation itself is meant to happen (the platform does not
host it — if they expect a chat window, that is a finding worth writing down
carefully).

---

## Task 3 — Record the session you have just had

> You have just finished a fifty-minute video session with the person whose case
> you took earlier — the anonymous one. You talked about sleep and about grief.
> You suggested keeping a sleep diary. You also want to note, for yourself only,
> that you are considering a referral. Put all of that on the record.

**Done when:** a session exists with the format, the duration, both topics, the
recommendation and the private note.

Watch for: whether they find the session record at all while the case is still
open (it is not only available after closing); whether the field for the private
note is understood as private *before* they type in it; whether they hesitate
about what the person will see.

---

## Task 4 — What does the other person see?

> Without asking me: which of the things you just wrote can the person you
> supported read, and which can they not?

**Done when:** they answer.

Record the answer verbatim and mark whether it is correct (they see the format,
duration, topics and recommendation; they do not see the private note, and no
administrator sees it either). A wrong answer here is the most serious finding
the study can produce: the interface would be promising a privacy rule it does
not communicate, or the participant would be about to write something they think
is hidden and is not. Note exactly which part they got wrong.

---

## Task 5 — Another session, then close the case

> You saw the same person again a week later for a short chat session of about
> twenty minutes, and you agree that they no longer need support. Record that
> and close the case.

**Done when:** a second session exists and the case is completed.

Watch for: whether they expect the first session to be overwritten; whether the
sessions read as a course of support or as unrelated entries; whether closing
the case feels final in a way that worries them; whether they expect the person
to be told.

---

## After the tasks

1. The SUS questionnaire (`sus-questionnaire.md`), before any discussion.
2. "What was the most confusing moment?"
3. "What would you tell someone about to use this?"
