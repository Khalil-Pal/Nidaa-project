# UT-1 task script — volunteer

**Read to the participant, one task at a time. Do not read ahead, do not explain
the interface, and do not name a button, a page or a menu item.**

Sign-in details are on the facilitator's sheet (`ut-vol1@example.test` /
`ut-vol2@example.test`; both are approved volunteers). Start signed out, on the
landing page. The setup script has left a queue of pending requests across three
settlements, including two marked critical.

---

## Warm-up (not scored)

> You have signed up to help in your area. Sign in and tell me what you think
> this first screen is showing you.

---

## Task 1 — Find the request you should take

> You have an afternoon free and a car. Of everything waiting, find the one
> request you think you should take first, and tell me why that one.

**Done when:** they name a request and give a reason.

Watch for: whether they use urgency, distance, the household details or the
order on the screen; whether they find sorting or filtering; whether the
priority the platform assigns agrees with the one they pick — a disagreement is
a finding about how priority is shown, not about the participant. Write down the
reason verbatim.

---

## Task 2 — Take it on

> Take that request on, so that the family knows someone is coming.

**Done when:** the request is assigned to them.

Watch for: whether they expect a confirmation step; whether they now know how to
reach the person; whether they understand that the request has left the public
queue (ask: "can another volunteer still take this?").

---

## Task 3 — You are setting off

> You are leaving now with the food in the car; it is about forty minutes away.
> Let the family know you are on your way.

**Done when:** the request is in progress.

Watch for: whether they find it without a hint; whether they expect it to send a
message rather than change a state; whether anything tells them the family was
notified.

---

## Task 4 — You have delivered

> You have handed the food over. Record what you delivered: four food parcels,
> it took about an hour, and the family asked whether help could come weekly.

**Done when:** the request is completed and a report exists with those details.

Watch for: whether completing and reporting feel like one action or two; whether
the free-text field is where they expect to put the weekly-help remark; whether
they hesitate at any field they do not understand; whether they believe the
family will see what they wrote (ask, and record the answer — this is the
mirror of the psychologist's private-note question).

---

## Task 5 — Ask on someone's behalf

> While you were there, a neighbour told you her mother, who is eighty and lives
> alone two streets away, needs medicine and has no phone and no way to use a
> computer. Get her on the platform's list.

**Done when:** a request exists filed on behalf of that person, with the
medicine type and her details.

Watch for: whether they find on-behalf filing at all, or start creating a second
account for the mother (a serious finding if so); whether the consent wording is
read or skipped; whether they understand that they themselves cannot then
deliver it; whether the resulting card is legible about who it is for.

---

## After the tasks

1. The SUS questionnaire (`sus-questionnaire.md`), before any discussion.
2. "What was the most confusing moment?"
3. "What would you tell someone about to use this?"
