# UT-1 task script — beneficiary

**Read to the participant, one task at a time. Do not read ahead, do not explain
the interface, and do not name a button, a page or a menu item anywhere in these
tasks: naming it would be doing the finding-it for them.**

Sign-in details for this session are on the facilitator's sheet
(`ut-bene1@example.test` / `ut-bene2@example.test`). Start each participant on
the landing page, signed out.

---

## Warm-up (not scored)

> You have heard about this platform from a neighbour. Have a look at the first
> page and tell me, in your own words, who you think it is for and what you
> could do with it.

Let them talk for a minute. Do not correct anything.

---

## Task 1 — Sign in and ask for food

> You are looking after a family of four, two of them children under ten. You
> have food for today and nothing for tomorrow. Sign in with the details on this
> card and ask for help.

**Done when:** a request exists with a type of food, four people, and children
marked.

Watch for: whether they find the way to start a request at all; whether they
understand that saying children are in the household changes how quickly they
are helped; whether urgency is chosen honestly or defensively (a participant who
picks "critical" for everything has read the scale as a volume dial, which is a
finding); whether the map or the address is used to say where they are.

---

## Task 2 — Ask for psychological support, without giving your name

> Things have been very hard since you were displaced and you have not been
> sleeping. You would like to talk to a professional, but you do not want the
> person to know who you are. Arrange that.

**Done when:** a psychological request exists with anonymity chosen.

Watch for: whether they find the psychological side of the platform at all (it
is a different page from material help — if they look for it inside the help
request form, that is a finding); whether they notice and trust the anonymity
option; whether they understand *what* stays hidden. Ask afterwards: "who do you
think can see your name now?" and write the answer down verbatim. This is the
single most important answer in the beneficiary script.

---

## Task 3 — Find out what is happening with your food request

> It is the next day. Has anyone taken on your request for food? What do you
> know about them?

**Done when:** they say, correctly, whether the request has been taken and by
whom (the facilitator will have accepted it between tasks — see the sheet).

Watch for: whether the state of the request is legible from the card alone, or
whether they have to open something; whether they find the responder's contact
details; whether they understand the difference between "assigned" and "on the
way".

---

## Task 4 — Say how the support session went

> You spoke to the psychologist yesterday. Tell the platform how it went: it was
> helpful, and you would give it four out of five.

**Done when:** a rating of four is recorded against the session.

Watch for: whether they find their way back to the case; whether they understand
that the session is a record kept by the psychologist and that they are rating
that session; whether they expect the psychologist to learn who rated them (ask,
and write the answer down).

---

## Task 5 — You no longer need the help

> A relative has brought you food for the week, so you no longer need the help
> you asked for. Sort that out on the platform.

**Done when:** the food request is cancelled.

Watch for: whether cancelling is findable; whether they hesitate because they
fear they are doing something rude or irreversible; whether the confirmation
makes clear what happens next (does the volunteer know?).

---

## After the tasks

1. The SUS questionnaire (`sus-questionnaire.md`), before any discussion.
2. "What was the most confusing moment?"
3. "What would you tell someone about to use this?"

Both answers verbatim on the observation sheet.
